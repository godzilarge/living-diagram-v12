# Round "grille", 2026-10-24: the Check view as free tiles

**Ask** (Orhan, 2026-10-24, correcting the tiles' round of 2026-10-21 after reading its three directions): not a
"bring to front" but "a grid, or a canvas, on which I can resize the tiles, something more personal to each taste:
some will want the verdicts larger (focus), others will concentrate on the coverage of the collection". A new
interaction, so a bench before any product code (`.claude/skills/brique-ui`, § 3). He asked for two directions on
react-grid-layout: **G1 bounded to the window** and **G2 the page scrolls**.

**Candidates** (built by the orchestrating session on react-grid-layout 2.3, legacy API, from `comps/shell/control.html`;
`README.md` has the detail, the gestures and the honest risks): **G1** a 12 × 24 board under the top row, tiles placed
freely without compaction, never overlapping, never off the board (a move into occupied space snaps back, a resize
stops at its neighbour; the board follows the window); **G2** 12 columns, 32 px rows, vertical compaction, the page
scrolls. Common: the header drags, eight resize handles, a dashed placeholder, 240 ms, container queries, "Agrandir"
on every tile, the layout remembered per direction, "Réinitialiser", a locked mode in the bench bar.

**Measured** (`capture.mjs`, Chromium 1228, 1440 × 900 and 1366 × 768, light and dark): every step holds the
16 / 16 px rule (16 px from the edges, 16 px between neighbours, no overlap). Lessons on the library: its
`preventCollision` keeps a dragged tile from following the pointer over occupied cells (it looked frozen on a full
board), its `isBounded` froze the drag, and a refused change leaves the tile where the pointer dropped it unless the
grid is remounted; the v2 component has no `allowOverlap` / `compactType`, the legacy API has them.

The bench lives in `bac-a-sable/grille-src/` (sources, `node_modules`) and `bac-a-sable/grille/` (the built page,
served at `/grille/`); this folder keeps the sources and the captures. `src/mockup.js` is generated by `build.py`.

**Orhan's first look** (2026-10-24, evening): G1 "you can never reorganize, unless it is a bug" (not a bug: a full
board without compaction cannot move before a tile shrinks; "Agrandir" is "really well handled"); G2: "I like the
freedom of placement a lot", but the drag "stutters, is visually choppy, the widgets struggle to find their place"
(continuous compaction, every tile animated 240 ms during the drag); both: the coverage widget is neat and readable,
the verdicts "too condensed, concentrated, lacking air" (the bench showed the mockup's 44 px register, not the
application's 56 px one). **G3** followed the same evening: the page scrolls, free placement without compaction nor
overlap (G1's board rule without a bottom), nothing else moves during a drag, the placeholder turns red where the
drop would be refused, "Resserrer" on demand; the verdicts are the application's register, with a second density
(64 px rows, 4 px between the lines, 44 px group headings, 32 px targets) to compare in the bench bar.

**Orhan's second look** (2026-10-24, night): "G3 is really not bad at all" (motion on the gestures to come in the
brick, with impeccable); the verdicts "better, but I am not convinced yet: the data does not look well organized in
the table, it lacks structure between the elements". Diagnosis: the number said twice (in the sentence and in the
count column), an identifier and a category mixed on the second line, nothing aligned, the grouping by severity
scattering one subject. **Five registers** in the bench bar on the same data (`src/verdicts.js`, `register.jsx`):
V0 the application's, V1 columns, V2 the number first, V3 by rule, V4 the sentence alone (README).

**Verdict**: waiting for Orhan: G3 confirmed in principle; which register, V0 to V4.

# star-atlas — challenger, competitive

**World**: the celestial star atlas: a deep navy chart ground, chalk-white stars whose size ranks their magnitude,
constellation lines and figures faint beneath the data, a coordinate grid, names set small, and the object under the
eye ringed in the red-amber of a night-vision flashlight.

**Thesis**: the infrastructure as a night chart: what matters is bigger, what connects is faint, what you are looking
at glows.

**Palette (commit to it; name the exact values in DIRECTION.md)**
- Ground: a **real navy** (blue, not a tinted black: think of the printed atlas plate), with a slightly lighter navy
  for bars and panel. This is the committed colour; it owns the whole surface.
- Marks and ink: chalk white, with secondary text tinted from the chalk toward the navy, never flat grey.
- **Night-vision red-amber** for the object under the eye: the selected device's ring, the current run, the focused
  control, the diff halo. One hue, one job.
- Cables as faint chalk lines (constellation lines), weight by speed class; documented-only dashed; down struck.
- State colours (error red distinct from the amber, warning, unreachable) only where they mean something; an
  unreachable device is a dimmed star with its ring broken.

**Type**: the chart's two voices: a humanist sans for the interface and device names (OFL: e.g. Source Sans 3,
Public Sans, Hanken Grotesk; **not** Inter, not IBM Plex), and **a light serif italic for the site / constellation
names only** (e.g. Source Serif 4 italic, Crimson Pro italic is on the avoid list, so prefer Source Serif 4 or
Literata italic). Tabular figures for coordinates and ports.

**Density**: medium; the canvas breathes, the panel is exact and compact.

**Signature move, concretely**: **importance as size on a fixed magnitude ramp**: a device's mark grows with its
cable count and role (the cores brightest and largest, access switches smaller, firewalls distinct), and **the sites
drawn as constellation figures**, a faint named region (`DC01`, `DC02` in the serif italic) under their devices,
like the mythic figure layer of a chart. The coordinate grid is faint and real (the plan's units).

**Hold the world to its four contributions**: no telescope, no compass rose, no star-chart ornament; standard chrome
(top bar or rail, panel, run band); device cards remain readable cards with the name, not bare dots: the magnitude
ramp sizes the card's mark and weight, the card stays a card.

**Scene**: dark (night operations on a laptop). Say how the light theme would go (the printed atlas: cream plate,
navy ink).

**Honest risk**: executed timidly this collapses into "dark UI with one accent", the exact rut PRODUCT.md rejects.
It must commit to navy, to the ramp, to the chart's typographic rigour (tabular figures, hairline grid, serif
italic held to the figures) to be a world and not a theme.
